# Why does everything I find on the internet read like spam?

Other possible titles:

## Intro

If you type simple questions into search engines and are surprised to frequently find long, drawn-out answers that barely touch your questions... and you suspect everybody else that writes web pages is just getting dumber or you want to know why... then this article might be for you.

Welcome to search-engine optimized articles. This post explains what is going on, why We Can't Have Nice Things Anymore, and why the internet used to be actually helpful for people researching stuff.

## Who writes web pages?

Do you blog? Do you write web pages about why your new XYZ vacuum is good and how to use it? Do you publish books about how to calculate the square root of 16? Probably not. Publishing used to be the purvue of nerds as an exposition of knowledge. But now, unfortunately, there is so much money at stake with e-commerce (at higher profit margins than other channels) that everything you read on the internet is influenced by the in-rush of SEO writers.

Search-engine optimization (SEO) writers are professionals that write seemingly-helpful web pages, and sometimes actually helpful web pages, with the sole agenda of promoting affiliate products for sale, promoting their own products for sale, or promoting the authority of their publication (again, to sell something).

I am writing about this because I know these topics well—in my day job I sell one of these high-margin online products that lives or dies by traffic from search engines—and also because I personally enjoy blogging. My motivation for writing this and other things on my blog is that it helps me organize my thoughts, maybe I'll meet interesting people for discussions on things I'm interested in. These discussions sometimes have lead to very good outcomes for me (typically those things are related to software I publish as open source).

**Key point: if you read something "informational" online, it was most likely written by somebody focused on promoting some related or unrelated thing for sale in a weird contrived way.**

## If I look up "do I have cancer" why can't the page just show me symptoms?

Because it does not follow the SEO format. The SEO format has a specific agenda and answering your questions directly does not promote their agenda.

## The SEO page agenda

**Simulate authority**

// if you ask a specific question like "is the iPhone XVIII worth the upgrade from the iPhone XVII?" then really you are hoping to read a page that:

- lists the differences
- provides a brief, useful insight
- is written by somebody that spent considerable time with both devices and represents your interest

Often SEO articles are written by brands that sell things, do not have your interest at heart, and are even published by authors that don't exist. i.e. they have a cute photo and have public profiles you can quickly find, but they don't ever get old and the photos are actually from other people that, most likely, allow their images to be used freely for any purpose without payment (i.e. free stock photography).

So what's the problem? Well the person writing the article needs to do certain things to make you forget that you are listening to an incompetent/inauthentic/ill-motivated writer. Some techniques include: dragging out the introduction more than 30 seconds worth of reading.

**Evergreen / don't ever rewrite**

**link authority // link to great non profits to make yourself look authoritative**

## The dark patterns

**Dragging out the introduction more than 30 seconds worth of reading.** This works because:

> **Postulate of the 30-second skeptic**. When you start reading a web page you are skeptical as to whether it is authoritative and helpful. If it was written by a XXX-policitally-leaning person but if you prefer reading from a YYY-politically-leaning person, you would abort. But after 30 seconds of continued reading, you forget that you were skeptical. If you find useful information then you assume that the information is authoritative (otherwise you would have already aborted). Not that you have information that is authoritative and useful, you can be compelled to act.
