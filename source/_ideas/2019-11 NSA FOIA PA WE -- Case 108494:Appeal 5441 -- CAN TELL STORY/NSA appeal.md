**Deletion at document A6692561 — “confidential source” and “C&R” — is unwarranted**

A confidential source is cited in the PG/SSBI FACTS SUMMARY. The entirety of this derragotry information is redacted with citation tho 5 U.S.C 552a(k)(5) and FOIA (b)(7)(D). However the law limits this exemption "to the extent that the disclosure of such material would reveal the identity of a source who furnished information to the Government under an express promise that the identity of the source would be held in confidence, or, prior to the effective date of this section, under an implied promise that the identity of the source would be held in confidence." 5 U.S.C 552a(k)(5).

- The agency response fails to include documentation of any express or implied promises made on behalf of the Government which are applicable. Such promises (if they were made) and documenttation of them would be critical and producible under requestors request.
- The agency response redacted the entire sentence. This is inappropriate because the redaction must only be to the "extent that the disclosure of such material would reveal the identity."

**Withholding of records — SF 86 and SSBI — is unwarranted**

The agency response cites several justifications for "deleting" and "excising" information from enclosures which were provided and only one justification for "witholding" records in their entirety. The justification for witholding entire records is provided by agency as (k)(6) / (b)(7)(E). Specifically, "testing or examination material used solely to determine individual qualifications for appointment or promotion in the Federal service the disclosure of which would compromise the objectivity or fairness of the testing or examination process." There is strong reason to believe that agency has access to additional records which have been inappropriately witheld and which do not fall under the agency's cited exemption of testing or examination. Specifically these include:

- The original and complete applicant Standard Form 86 Questionairre.
- SSBI report.

The "SSBI" report is summarized on the "FACTS SUMMARY" document. This provides a strong reason to believe that the agency has the underlying detailed SSBI report in its records. Such a report is in scope of the requestor's request and it was inappropriately witheld by the agency without justification.

**Deletion / excision of examination questions — while published on SF 86 — is unwarranted**

The agency response deletes/excises all examination questions from the Report of Polygraph Examination (May 9, 2005 and March 18, 2005 documents). Agency justification for these exemptions is cited as (b)(7)(e) which applies to records which "would disclose guidelines for law enforcement investigations or prosecutions if such disclosure could reasonably be expected to risk circumvention of the law." The requestor has firsthand knowledge of this activity since he is the subject of the polygraph examination. As such, he is aware that the redacted questions are actually the same questions which are published in U.S. Office of Personnel Management Standard Form 86, Questionairre for National Security Positions. For example, SF-86 question 29.1. Whereas the Government already publishes these specific questions in the context of "background investigations for national security positions" (SF-86), disclosure of the same or substantially similar questions in the agency response (relating to a background investigation for a national security position) could not reasonably be expected to risk circumvention of the law. Therefore the cited exemption is not applicable and the agency response is decificent for deleting/excising this information.

Once a conditional offer of employment has been extended, **employers may ask only about criminal convictions**. Once a conditional offer of employment has been made, employers may only withdraw the offer of employment or take adverse action in limited circumstances (§32-1342 of the DC Code).
