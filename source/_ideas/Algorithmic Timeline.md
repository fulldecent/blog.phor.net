# Algorithmic Timeline

INTRO:

"I HATE XXXX PEOPLE". That's an opinion and it doesn't hurt anybody.

In the beginning of the internet, anybody could say this, say what they really feel. They could say it, let it go, learn from it. and move on. Everything was ephemeral. Most things were anonymous.

But now it's hurting people, why? Obviously because clickbait, and endorphins and peer pressure—the things that sell ads on today's "free"-to-use online platforms. But let's see a little lower, the mechanics of why hate, and anything else you can say, is hurtful specifically on today's internet.

In the past, when you say "I HATE XXXX PEOPLE", or "I like Pepsi" or anything else (on an epheremal, mostly anonymous internet) that was an opinion. But today with the permanent record, and real-name policy internet, this is an identity.

According to *Atomic Habits*, an identity is how you build habits and how you ossify behaviors.

| Past                | Now                               |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| You **liked** Pepsi | You **are** a Pepsi-loving person |

And anything you say in the future can be tied back to your Pepsi-loving-person past. What about when you learn that Pepsi is a piece of shit and basically gives everybody diabetes? Well, now you're just a Pepsi-disliking Pepsi-lover. When you tell people about what you learned, other people can just scroll up and quote-retweet your old message (either the original or the copy on Archive.org).

With an identity you must always reconcile how you feel now with your old identity. That's bullshit. It's not human. It's not wholesome. But it makes for great debates.

The whole point of today's "free"-to-use online platforms is to generate engagement—i.e. debate, endorphins, anything that gets you excited.

## What do we go from here?

If you are a "free"-to-use platform, continue to exploit people and make them fight untennable positions. Take anything somebody said in the past, put their photo next to it, and put that snippet right next to anything new they say that is related but contradictory. Watch them punch themself out of that paper bag in full public view. Stir up shit by amplifying that snippet to close personal relationships of that person like their boss, people colocate with at dinner time on holidays, and new connections.

If you are an individual, hold no loyalty to your past self. Forgive. And feel no obligation to justify or caveat. More importantly, when you see things other people wrote, always assume they are quoting somebody else. Don't assume anybody's intentional are sincere unless you are having a face-to-face conversation with them today. Even people you spoke with yesterday are not the same if you see them again today. That's the beautiful thing about humans.
