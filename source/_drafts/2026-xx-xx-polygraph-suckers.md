This was part of my employment screening for the US National Security Agency (NSA). And this agency used the FBI for my polygraph exams. This was on-site at Fort Meade, Maryland.

## The actual questions

The administrator asked me some "irrelevant" questions. I see no damage to the state to disclose some of them here since according to the NSA records below they are not relevant to the exam.

These questions include:

- Is your name "William Entriken Jr"?
- Were you born on XX XX, XXXX?

As for the counterintelligence questions, I did not agree to keep them confidential. As a courtesy, I will not publish the exact wording they used. But to give you an understanding, here is a flavor of what these questions looked like:

- Did you ever murder a US Senator in an intentional attempt to violently overthrow the US government?

You can see they only used seven and then ten questions of these kinds. Most of these were duplicates. Across both interviews, the total number of actual questions like this fit on one hand. And then three more ad-lib questions they added.

Guess which one was on my mind the whole time.

## A polygraph gone wrong

The first polygraph didn't go well. It lasted some hours and my outcome was: counterintelligence inconclusive, lifestyle no significant response.

This session started at 0800. And, notably, this is the first day in my life I drank coffee.

Questions like "what is your name?" and "do you shoot senators?" asked repeatedly over the course of multiple hours, for multiple days, and multiple session each day elicit a thought and physiological response from me that has nothing to do with my truthful YES / NO answers to these questions.

Initially I was vividly focused on:

- How would it feel to sit next to a US senator that somebody else was assisinating?
- Why did my parents pick my name?
- Nasty thoughts about the cute FBI girl I was locked in a room with for most of the day.

And then you know a polygraph is going poorly when the operator asks you:

- Operator: "Were you born on XX XX, XXXX?"
- You: "Yes."
- Long delay.
- Operator: "Um, are you sure?"

She showed my how bad my readout looked and said I was giving her "crap". She told me to stop breathing in so much. And stop delaying between breaths. And stop moving in my chain. Basically this was also my first yoga class.

From then I tried to dull my mind by going into the breathing trance she required and then only thinking about:

- Does the light switch on the wall actually have sharp edges or are they rounded?
- How rounded?
- Is the rounding detectable by the limits of my visual acuity?
- Or is my visual acuity detiorating while I remain trained on this one spot?
- I just answered a question, breath in for three count and don't wait too long to exhale.

## Engineers can't do polygraphs

The other half of my brain was fully focused on one question. And when I realized that everybody probably got the same questions as me, it unlocked. Everybody they hired is an idiot and I am a liar!

SPOILER ALERT.

In a magnificent revelation, I realized that "yes" is not the correct answer to "were you born on XX XX, XXXX?" In fact, a "yes" answer by anybody is always a lie. Because the correct answer to that question is always "I don't remember!"

I filed this next to "you can't do that!", another prima facie contradiction for my collection.

Engineers are interested in these things. Any engineer who passes this test is a liar. And this contradiction/lie is the primary thing I contemplated for hours, through both sessions.

## Crimes

After the first horrible yoga session, they traveled me back to Fort Meade to do it again. This time, I didn't drink coffee.

When the operator asked about my birthday, I gave the canonical lie again.

The again the operator told me how bad my readout looked and asked if there were any crimes I needed to confess to. "No" was not accepted so I invented a couple low-value transgressions that I didn't actually commit and which might not be crimes. Don't do that.

After more hours of the same thing. The results came back worse: counterintelligence inconclusive, lifestyle inconclusive.

I had scored "inconclusive" on every part of the exam, including apparently the part they weren't scoring.

## End result

The NSA was preparing to invite me back for a third polygraph. But in the meantime a confidential informant told them that I [broke into the servers at my college](/best-language-for-illicit-code) (which I didn't). And they ended my employment consideration.

Later, China broke in and stole the applications of all Tier 5+ investigation candidates (TOP SECRET / SCI). I'm on that list and got free credit monitoring. But China did NOT steal the list of people that dropped out. So they think I'm a spy. I also travel extensively, speak mandarin and work with random governments on vague projects.
